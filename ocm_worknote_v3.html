Code: phase11_condition_baseline.py, phase12_runs_and_coverage.py, phase8b_label_sweep.py, phase13_condition_value.py, phase14_crossprep_pft.py. Every number below is stored in the JSON file of the same name.

Protocol. Unchanged from v2. All measurements of a catalyst stay in one fold. Five repeats with different fold assignments. A difference between two models is reported with a 95% confidence interval from resampling catalysts.

1. What the condition data settled

  • Each catalyst was tested at 5 temperatures (700–900 °C) and 27 gas-flow conditions per temperature.
  • The 27 conditions form a full 3 × 3 × 3 design:
    • total flow of 10, 15 or 20 (in the units recorded in the file)
    • Ar fraction of 0.70, 0.40 or 0.15
    • CH₄:O₂ ratio of 2, 4 or 6
  • The readings within one temperature are therefore 27 distinct conditions. Version 2 (§5) called them near-identical readings. That description was wrong. The training label is unchanged (§5 below). Only the reason for it changes.
  • Incomplete groups come from runs excluded for poor mass balance, as you explained. This answers both questions in version 2 (§8).

2. Prediction of individual measurements

Adding the three gas-flow columns lowers the prediction error for unseen catalysts:

  • RMSE falls from 2.90 to 2.66, a reduction of 8.1%.
  • The 95% confidence interval of the reduction is 0.22 to 0.26.

Most of the gain comes from predicting how yield changes between conditions at the same temperature.

3. Ranking of catalysts

The conditions do not improve the ranking of catalysts by best yield.

  • A fair comparison needs the same conditions for every catalyst. We used the 18 conditions with CH₄:O₂ = 2 at 800 and 850 °C. 465 catalysts were measured at all of them. We chose this set by measurement coverage alone, before comparing any models.
  • The change in rank correlation is +0.005 (95% CI −0.007 to +0.015). We find no detectable change.
  • Scoring each catalyst only over the conditions that passed the mass-balance check shows an apparent gain. That gain disappears on catalysts with comparable measurement effort, so we do not report it.

4. Where the conditions add value

Choosing conditions for a new catalyst. The model ranks a new catalyst's 135 conditions (5 temperatures × 27).

  • The model's advantage is 0.15 yield points (95% CI 0.07 to 0.23). It holds in all five repeats.
  • The effect is consistent but small: about 1.4% of the mean best yield (10.4%).
  • A chosen condition that was never measured counts as a failed run.

A reduced measurement set. We chose 10 of the 135 conditions, using only training catalysts: the 10 that most often give a training catalyst its best yield.

  • On held-out catalysts, the best yield over these 10 conditions ranks catalysts with a rank correlation of 0.969 against the full data. This is the mean over five repeats; 92% of catalysts had at least one of the 10 measured.
  • Ten random conditions reach at most 0.925 (97.5th percentile).
  • This estimate is optimistic. The full-data best yield comes from the same runs.
  • A ranking model trained on 10-condition labels shows no detectable loss (−0.009, 95% CI −0.018 to +0.001).

Per-catalyst response models. A model that predicts each catalyst's response to the conditions directly did not improve on the current model. The current model already captures most of the learnable pattern, so we have stopped this line.

5. Training label

We keep the per-temperature median, then the highest of these medians, as stated in v2.

  • Reason. The label rests on many readings, not one. Removing each catalyst's single highest reading moves the median label by 0.01 yield points. It moves the maximum by 0.38 points. Both figures are medians over catalysts.
  • Ranking. The median versus the maximum: +0.005 (95% CI −0.002 to +0.012), no detectable difference. The upper quartile: +0.006 (95% CI +0.001 to +0.012).
  • One caution. 84.5% of catalysts reach their highest yield at CH₄:O₂ = 2, the lowest ratio tested. For most catalysts the best yield therefore lies at the edge of the tested conditions.

6. Literature data across preparation methods

In v2, a plain merge of laboratory and impregnation literature data ranked non-impregnation literature better than our two-stage model (rank correlation 0.388 versus 0.318).

  • Two-stage designs. We tested three further designs, including one in which every stage-1 prediction comes from a model that did not see that row. None ranked better than the plain merge. Two ranked lower, and one showed no detectable difference.
  • Label-shift correction. One element of our two-stage method does help. Before merging, the literature yields are rescaled onto the laboratory yield distribution (quantile normalisation). Rank correlation rises from 0.388 to 0.422. The difference is +0.034 (95% CI +0.018 to +0.050), and it holds in all five repeats and under the median label.
  • Enrichment of top performers shows no detectable change.
  • Status. This comparison was added during the analysis, so we treat it as exploratory.
  • Scope. The literature file has no condition columns, so the gas-flow data cannot enter this part.

7. Two observations on the data

Catalysts with a second set of runs. - For 28 catalysts, 1,906 conditions appear twice. - At a given temperature, one set is higher at every condition, by a median factor of 2.3. Both sets follow the same pattern across conditions. - 10 of the 92 top-decile catalysts are among these 28; chance would give 2.8. If the paired readings are averaged, 4 remain.

Excluded runs. - Exclusions are more frequent for weaker catalysts. The rank correlation between a catalyst's number of missing conditions (out of 135) and its best yield is −0.29. - Within a catalyst, the excluded conditions do not appear to be its low-yield ones. The difference fell below our pre-set threshold for all three CH₄:O₂ ratios.
